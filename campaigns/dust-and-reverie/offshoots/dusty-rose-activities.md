# The Dusty Rose - Activities & Distractions

> *"The Rose ain't just a saloon. It's where Dusthollow comes to forget its troubles. Or make new ones."*

Use this document when players want to explore the Dusty Rose beyond the main plot. These activities add flavor, opportunities for subtle glitches, and NPC interactions.

---

## The Bar

### Ordering Drinks

**The Bartender: Silas**
A heavyset man with a walrus mustache and permanently tired eyes. He's been pouring drinks "as long as he can remember" (literally—he has no memories before the Reach).

**Drink Menu**:
| Drink | Price | Description |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| Whiskey | 5 cp | Burns going down. The house standard. |
| Beer | 2 cp | Flat, warm, gets the job done. |
| "Sylene's Reserve" | 2 sp | Smoother whiskey. Actually the same bottle. |
| Dusty Rose Special | 5 sp | Whiskey with honey and something spicy. |
| Sarsaparilla | 1 cp | For the temperate. Or the young. |
| "Whatever you got" | 3 cp | Silas's choice. Usually whiskey. |

**Silas's Dialogue**:
- "What'll it be?"
- "Tab's due end of the week. Don't make me remind you again."
- "You look like you've had a long ride. Whiskey fixes that."
- *If asked about himself*: "Me? I've been here... well, long as the Rose has stood, I reckon. Don't think about it much."
- *If asked about strangers*: "Folks come and go. Some stay. Some don't. Best not to ask too many questions in a place like this."

**Glitch Opportunity** (DC 15 Perception):
Silas pours the exact same pour—down to the angle of the bottle and the timing—every single time. Like a practiced motion repeated thousands of times.

## The Card Tables

Three card tables occupy the back corner. Games run from morning till well past midnight.

### Playing Cards

**The Game**: Five-Card Stud or Frontier Hold 'Em (use poker rules, or simplify to opposed checks)

**Simple Mechanic**:
- Ante: 1 sp minimum, 1 gp at the "high stakes" table
- Each round: Opposed Wisdom (Insight) vs. Charisma (Deception) checks
- Winner takes the pot
- Cheating: Sleight of Hand (DC 14) to succeed, opposed by Perception to catch

**Regular Players**:

**"Lucky" Mercer** (gambler, regular)
- Thin man, quick hands, nervous laugh
- Always wins the first hand, loses the rest
- *Dialogue*: "Care to try your luck? I'm feeling generous today."
- *Hidden*: His "luck" is programmed. He wins early to hook players, then loses to make them feel good.

**Hollis Tate** (rancher, regular)
- Heavyset, ruddy-faced, loud
- Bad at cards, doesn't care, here for the company
- *Dialogue*: "I'm down six bits but I'm up two friends. That's a profit in my book!"
- *If losing badly*: "Well, that's my wife's egg money. She's gonna tan my hide."

**Clara Vance** (widow, occasional)
- Sharp-eyed, quiet, dressed in faded black
- Actually a skilled player who hides it
- *Dialogue*: "I'll see your bet." *Long pause.* "And raise."
- *Glitch*: She sometimes stares at the cards with a flicker of confusion, as if she's seen this exact hand before.

**The Card Game That Turns Violent**
Per the narrative Thread, a card game goes bad each day around midday. Hollis accuses Lucky of cheating (he's not wrong—Lucky's Thread includes subtle cheating that he's unaware of). If not interrupted:
- Hollis throws a punch
- Lucky draws a knife
- Someone gets hurt
- Sheriff-less town means no consequences

**PC Intervention Options**:
- Talk Hollis down (DC 12 Persuasion)
- Reveal Lucky's cheating (DC 14 Perception to spot, then roleplay)
- Let it play out (Hollis takes a knife wound, recovers by tomorrow)

## The Piano

### The Piano Player: Emmett

A gaunt man with thinning hair and nicotine-stained fingers. He plays the same rotation of songs endlessly. He doesn't take requests—or rather, he *can't*.

**His Rotation** (loops every 45 minutes):
1. "My Darling Clementine"
2. A melancholy waltz (untitled)
3. An upbeat dance number
4. "Streets of Laredo" (funeral song)
5. The waltz again
6. Silence for exactly two minutes
7. Repeat

**Interacting with Emmett**:

*If players request a song*:
> Emmett's fingers pause for just a moment. His eyes flicker—something almost like recognition. Then his hands resume, playing the next song in his rotation as if you hadn't spoken.

*If players insist or get aggressive*:
> He looks at you with genuine confusion. "I... I don't..." His hands twitch toward the keys. "I only know these ones, friend. Always have." He sounds uncertain, like he's never been asked before.

*If players physically stop him from playing*:
> The silence feels *wrong*. The whole saloon seems to hold its breath. Emmett stares at his hands like he doesn't know what to do with them. After a long, uncomfortable moment, [[npcs/sylene|Sylene]] appears. "Let him play," she says quietly. "He gets... confused otherwise."

**Spiral Point**: +1 if players push Emmett and witness his confusion

**Glitch Opportunity**:
If players watch Emmett for more than 10 minutes, DC 14 Perception notices:
- He plays the exact same wrong note in "Clementine" every time
- His fingers sometimes move a half-second before the music sounds
- During the two-minute silence, he doesn't blink

## The Working Girls

The Dusty Rose's upper floor hosts "entertainment." [[npcs/sylene|Sylene]] runs this operation with firm rules and genuine care for her employees.

### The Women

**Ruby** (the bold one)
- Red hair (dyed), freckles, loud laugh
- Forward but not pushy
- *Approach*: "Well now, look what the wind blew in. You look like you could use some company, stranger."
- *If declined politely*: "Your loss, handsome. I'll be around if you change your mind."
- *If declined rudely*: "Suit yourself. Some men don't know quality when they see it." *Returns to her drink, unbothered.*

**Pearl** (the quiet one)
- Pale, dark-haired, watchful
- Hangs back, lets others approach
- *If approached*: "You're not from around here." *A statement, not a question.* "I can always tell."
- *Hidden*: Pearl notices things. She's seen the Ashen Rider come and go for years. She knows something's wrong but can't articulate it.

**Delilah** (the experienced one)
- Older than the others, elegant, knows everyone
- Acts as den mother to the younger women
- *Dialogue*: "First time at the Rose? Let me buy you a drink. Sylene likes to know who's new in town."
- *If asked about the town*: "Dusthollow's a good place. Quiet. Same faces, same stories. Some folks find that comforting."

### Flirting Mechanics

Players can flirt, buy drinks, dance, or converse without purchasing services.

**Buying a Drink for a Working Girl**: 1 sp (includes her company for conversation)

**Dancing**: Free, happens during evening hours when Emmett plays the upbeat number

**Conversation Topics**:
- Town gossip (who's new, who's trouble, who's sweet on whom)
- The man in black (they've all seen him; he never partakes, just watches)
- Strange happenings (played off as "frontier superstition")
- Their lives (backstories that don't quite hold up to scrutiny)

**Sylene's Rules** (enforced):
- No violence toward the women
- Payment is private, upstairs only
- What happens at the Rose stays at the Rose

*If players push for more than flirtation*:
> Ruby laughs and pats your cheek. "Buy a girl dinner first. And by dinner, I mean several more drinks and a private conversation with Madame Sylene about her rates."

> Sylene appears as if summoned. "My girls aren't trophies, stranger. If you're looking for company, we can discuss terms. If you're looking to cause trouble, the door's that way."

**Glitch Opportunity** (DC 16 Insight, conversation with any of them):
They all have the same gap in their stories—none of them can remember how they came to Dusthollow. They arrived "a few years back" with vague references to somewhere else.

## The Saloon Floor - Ambient NPCs

These patrons populate the Dusty Rose and can be interacted with for flavor.

### Named Patrons

**Old Jem**
- Ancient prospector, mostly deaf, nursing a beer
- Tells rambling stories about "the old days" that contradict each other
- *Dialogue*: "Back in sixty-two—or was it fifty-eight?—I found a vein of silver wide as my arm. Then the mountain ate it. Swallowed it right up. Mountains do that, y'know."

**The Garrett Brothers (Tom and Billy)**
- Cowboys, work the range outside town
- Loud, friendly, always arguing about nothing
- *Tom*: "I'm telling you, the bay mare's faster."
- *Billy*: "And I'm telling *you*, you're an idiot. The roan's got legs like—"
- *Both, if interrupted*: "Stay outta this, stranger. This is family business." *Then immediately forget what they were arguing about.*

**Widow Parsons**
- Elderly woman in black, sips sherry alone
- Stares out the window like she's waiting for someone
- *If approached*: "Have you seen my husband? He went out this morning and..." *She trails off.* "What was I saying?"
- *Hidden*: Her husband "died" years ago in the narrative. She keeps waiting for him. He resets each morning but never comes back to her.

**Father Aldous** (from the chapel)
- Drinks more than a preacher should
- Quotes scripture that isn't quite right
- *Dialogue*: "The good book says, 'Round and round the path leads, but the center waits for those who seek.' Or something like that."
- *Hidden*: He's quoting spiral imagery Vane embedded in his programming.

### Ambient Actions (Roll d8 or choose)

| d8 | What's Happening |
|----|------------------|
| 1 | Tom and Billy start arguing. It escalates to arm wrestling. |
| 2 | Ruby laughs loudly at something a cowboy said. |
| 3 | Old Jem mutters about "the thing in the mine" then falls asleep. |
| 4 | The card game gets tense. Someone accuses someone of cheating. |
| 5 | Emmett plays the wrong note. A beat of silence. He continues. |
| 6 | Sylene makes rounds, speaking quietly to each table. |
| 7 | A stranger enters, orders a drink, leaves without finishing it. |
| 8 | Everyone goes quiet for a moment. Then conversation resumes as if nothing happened. |

## Hidden Details for DMs

### Patterns to Plant
- Same people sit in the same seats each day
- Conversations repeat if players listen long enough
- The "stranger" in Ambient Action #7 is always the same man (a background Woven on a minimal Thread)

### Glitch Seeds
- Silas's identical pour
- Emmett's inability to deviate
- The working girls' missing arrival memories
- Widow Parsons' eternal wait
- Father Aldous's spiral scripture

### Patron Tells
If a Patron (guest) is present:
- They never buy their own drinks (Woven serve them automatically)
- They speak slightly "wrong" (anachronistic phrases, modern cadence)
- Violence directed at them "misses" or gets deflected
- They treat Woven with casual familiarity, like old toys

## Time Progression

If players spend significant time at the Rose:

| Time | Event |
|------|-------|
| Morning | Quiet. Regulars nursing hair-of-the-dog. Silas cleaning glasses. |
| Midday | Card game heats up. The violent argument brewing. |
| Afternoon | Post-confrontation lull. New faces drift in. |
| Evening | Dance time. Emmett plays upbeat. Ruby works the floor. |
| Night | Drinking in earnest. Secrets spilled. Quiet conversations. |
| Late Night | Winding down. Old Jem snoring. Last calls. |

Players who stay through the full cycle may notice it repeats almost exactly the next day.
