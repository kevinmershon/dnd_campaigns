## The Walk Into Town

### Read-Aloud: Main Street

> The stagecoach rolls away, leaving you standing in a cloud of settling dust. Main Street stretches before you—a single rutted road flanked by wooden buildings that lean like tired old men.
>
> To your left, a general store with a sagging porch. To your right, a livery stable where a old man brushes down a roan mare. Further along, you see the painted sign of a saloon—"The Dusty Rose"—and beyond it, a church steeple catches the afternoon light.
>
> The town smells of horse, dust, and something cooking. A dog sleeps in a patch of shade. Two men argue about a horse. A woman hangs laundry behind a boarding house.
>
> It feels like a place where nothing much happens. And yet, something about the golden light, the too-perfect quiet, makes the hair on your neck prickle.

### Ambient Details (Use as needed)

| Sense | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Sight | Dust motes floating in sunbeams. A weathervane shaped like a rooster, spinning lazily. Wanted posters peeling on a board. |
| Sound | Distant piano music. A hammer on an anvil. The creak of a rocking chair. Someone whistling the same tune as the piano. |
| Smell | Horse manure. Fresh bread. Tobacco smoke drifting from a porch. |
| Touch | The dry heat. Grit between your teeth. The worn smoothness of the boardwalk rail. |

### Glitch Seed (Optional, DC 16 Perception)
> The same mangy dog is sleeping in two different patches of shade on opposite ends of the street.

## Locations Along Main Street

### Harken's General Store

**Status**: Open, welcoming

> A cramped shop stuffed with everything a frontier town might need. Rope coiled on barrels. Canned goods stacked to the ceiling. A glass case of ammunition. The smell of leather and dried beans.

**[[characters/npcs/dusthollow-townsfolk#Harken|Harken]]**: Portly, balding, perpetually cheerful. Wipes his hands on his apron constantly.

*Dialogue*:

### The Sheriff's Office

**Status**: Closed, but unlocked

> A squat building with iron bars on the windows. A faded star painted on the door. The "OPEN" sign has been flipped to "CLOSED" and looks like it's been that way for a while.

**Inside** (if players enter):
> Dust on everything. Two empty cells. A desk with old paperwork. A gun rack—empty except for a single rusty shotgun. A calendar on the wall shows last month's date.

*If players investigate*:
- DC 12 Investigation: The sheriff's badge is in a desk drawer. No one's claimed it.
- DC 14 Investigation: A half-written letter: "...strange happenings continue. The same incidents repeat. I'm starting to think—" It ends mid-sentence.
- DC 16 Investigation: Scratch marks on the inside of one cell door. Someone counted days. The count reaches 47 and stops.

**What Happened**: The previous sheriff started noticing patterns. He was "pulled for maintenance" (the Consortium removed him). No one's mentioned him since.

**Why It's Restrictive**: No one's here. It's dusty and abandoned. Players can poke around but there's no one to talk to and the clues are subtle.

### Doc Mercer's Practice

**Status**: Open, but the doc is busy

> A narrow building with a painted sign: "E. MERCER, M.D." Through the window, you see a man in a vest bandaging someone's arm.

**[[characters/npcs/dusthollow-townsfolk#Doc Mercer|Doc Mercer]]**: Tired eyes, steady hands, doesn't waste words.

*If players enter*:
> The doctor looks up briefly. "Unless you're bleeding, you'll have to wait. Got a man here who tangled with barbed wire."

*If players insist or wait*:
- "What can I do for you? I do extractions, set bones, stitch wounds. Payment up front."
- *If asked about strange happenings*: "People get hurt. People get better. That's all I see." *He's lying—he's noticed that injuries heal overnight. He doesn't want to think about it.*
- *If pressed (DC 15 Persuasion)*: "Look, I don't ask questions. Man comes in with a bullet wound on Monday, he's fine Tuesday. I write it off as frontier toughness. You should too."

**Why It's Restrictive**: He's busy and brusque. Getting anything useful requires pushing, and even then he deflects.

### The Dusthollow Chapel

**Status**: Open, but empty (Father Aldous is at the Rose)

> A simple wooden church. White paint peeling. A bell tower that's never rung in living memory. The door creaks when you push it open.

**Inside**:
> Rows of worn pews. A simple altar. Afternoon light through stained glass that depicts... a strange pattern. Not quite religious imagery. Something spiral-shaped worked into the design.

*If players investigate*:
- DC 10 Religion: The iconography is unusual. It doesn't match any major faith.
- DC 14 Investigation: Hymnals contain songs with strange lyrics. "Round and round the shepherd walks / The flock forgets, the shepherd talks."
- DC 16 Perception: The stained glass spiral seems to shift slightly when you're not looking directly at it.

**[[characters/npcs/dusthollow-townsfolk#Father Aldous|Father Aldous]]**: Not here. Can be found at the Dusty Rose, drinking more than a preacher should.

**Why It's Less Interesting**: Empty. Atmospheric but no interaction. The clues here are very subtle—more payoff in later sessions when the spiral means something.

### Brennan's Livery

**Status**: Open

> The smell of hay and horse. A large stable with a corral behind it. An old man with a face like weathered leather is brushing down a mare.

**[[characters/npcs/dusthollow-townsfolk#Old Brennan|Old Brennan]]**: Half-deaf, gruff, knows more than he lets on.

*Dialogue* (speak loudly):
- "Eh? What's that? Speak up!"
- "Horses? Got 'em. Rooms? Try the Rose. Food? Try the Rose. Everything else? Try the Rose."
- *If asked about strangers*: "Folks come and go. Don't pay much attention. 'Cept that one in black. He comes through regular-like. Never boards a horse. Don't know how he travels."
- *If asked about the town*: "Been here long as I can remember. Which ain't saying much these days."

**Why It's Restrictive**: Brennan's deafness makes conversation tedious. He redirects to the Rose. Useful for horses, not much else.

### The Boarding House (Ma Pickett's)

**Status**: Technically open, but unwelcoming

> A two-story building with lace curtains and a "ROOMS" sign. A severe-looking woman in black sits on the porch, watching the street with disapproval.

**[[characters/npcs/dusthollow-townsfolk#Ma Pickett|Ma Pickett]]**: Widow. Runs a "respectable" establishment. Hates the Dusty Rose and everything it stands for.

*If players approach*:
> "I run a *respectable* house. No drinking. No smoking. No cursing. No... *company*." She eyes you with suspicion. "You look like trouble. The Rose is that way."

*If players insist on a room*:
- 1 gp/night. Curfew at 9 PM. No exceptions.
- Breakfast included (porridge, always porridge).
- She monitors hallways. No privacy.

*Dialogue*:
- "This town's gone to sin and I'm the only one who sees it."
- "That Sylene woman runs a house of ill repute. Sheriff should shut it down. If we had a sheriff."
- *If asked about strange things*: "The only strange thing is how folks accept wickedness as normal."

**Why It's Restrictive**: Ma Pickett is actively unpleasant. Staying here means curfews and surveillance. Most players will prefer the Rose.

### The Blacksmith (Cade's Forge)

**Status**: Open, working

> The ring of hammer on anvil. Heat shimmers from the forge. A massive man with arms like tree trunks shapes a horseshoe.

**[[characters/npcs/dusthollow-townsfolk#Cade|Cade]]**: Doesn't talk while working. Which is always.

*If players wait for him to finish* (takes 10 minutes):
- "Need something forged? Horseshoes, tools, hinges. Nothing fancy."
- *If asked about weapons*: "Don't make weapons. Don't need 'em." *He's lying—he made the sheriff's star. He doesn't want to remember.*
- *If asked about the town*: "It's a town. People live here. What else you want to know?"

**What's Available**: Basic metal goods. No weapons. Repairs to equipment.

**Why It's Restrictive**: Cade barely talks. The forge is hot and loud. There's nothing to learn here.

### The Water Tower

**Status**: Accessible (ladder is rickety)

> The tallest structure in town. A wooden tank on a skeletal frame. A ladder leads up, missing a few rungs.

*If players climb* (DC 10 Athletics):
> From up here, you can see the whole town laid out below. The street, the buildings, the people going about their business. Beyond the town limits, grassland stretches in every direction—and at the horizon, the world seems to... shimmer. Like heat haze, but wrong.

- DC 14 Perception: The shimmer isn't random. It forms patterns. Almost geometric.
- DC 16 Perception (at night): There are no stars. The sky is simply black.

**Why It's Less Interesting**: It's a water tower. The view is nice. The clues are there but subtle—players need to be looking.

---

### The Well (Town Center)

**Status**: Accessible

> A stone well with a wooden cover. A bucket on a rope. Nothing special—except the echoes.

*If players lower the bucket or speak into the well*:
> Your voice echoes back strangely. Not a simple reflection—words that sound almost like yours, but not quite. Different inflections. Different emotions. A conversation with yourself that you never had.

- DC 12 Perception: The echoes don't match what you said.
- DC 15 Insight: They sound like *memories*. Of things you said before. Or will say.
- If players persist, they might hear: "...don't trust him, he's not what he seems..." or "...we've been here before, haven't we?..." in their own voice.

**Spiral Point**: +1 for engaging with the well and hearing the echoes

## Encounter: The Garrett Brothers

As players walk through town, they pass the Garrett brothers arguing:

> Two cowboys face each other in the middle of the street, gesticulating wildly. A bay horse and a roan stand nearby, looking bored.
>
> "The bay's faster and you know it!"
> "The bay couldn't outrun a lame mule!"

*If players intervene*:
- They both turn, annoyed. "This is family business, stranger."
- If players offer to settle it (a race, a bet), the brothers consider it, then refuse. "Nah. We'll figure it out ourselves. Always do."
- They immediately return to arguing, using the exact same words.

**Glitch Seed** (DC 14 Perception): They repeat the argument word-for-word every few minutes. Same gestures, same inflections.

## Where Players End Up

Every path leads to the Rose:
- **Harken** suggests it for rooms and food
- **Brennan** redirects everything there
- **Ma Pickett** forbids it (making it more attractive)
- **Cade** doesn't care but mentions it's where people go
- **The sheriff's office** is empty—questions lead to "ask at the saloon"
- **The chapel** is empty—Father Aldous is at the Rose

This isn't railroading—it's the town's natural gravity. The Rose is the social hub. Everything else is just set dressing (for now).

## Optional: The Stagecoach Leaves

If players look back:

> The stagecoach is already gone. You didn't hear it leave. It was there a moment ago, and now the street is empty. No dust trail. No hoofprints leading out.
>
> Just the quiet afternoon and the feeling that you've been here longer than you think.

**Spiral Point**: +1 for noticing and commenting on this
